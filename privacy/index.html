Vocablora

Privacy policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

Türkçe

Your decks and settings stay on your device. The Vocablora app has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no cookies. It connects to the internet only when you choose to create cards with AI, open these legal pages or write to us.

1. Who we are

Vocablora is a vocabulary flashcard app made by Arda Yıldız, who is the data controller for it. You can reach us at app.ardayildiz@gmail.com.

This policy covers the Vocablora app distributed through the App Store and Google Play.

2. What stays on your device

Vocablora stores the following in the app's own storage on your device. It is never sent to us, and we cannot see it.

  • Your decks: their names, languages, level, words, meanings and examples, and when they were created and changed.
  • Your settings: the app language, theme, deck layout, study method, the learning and meaning languages and the level of the last deck you created (the defaults for your next deck), your AI card creation permission setting (off by default), whether haptics are on, and the daily reminder: whether it is on and its time.
  • An unfinished deck you are adding, or unfinished words you are adding to a deck, so they are not lost while you switch to a chat app. They are removed when you save or discard them.
  • Which screen you were last on, so the app can reopen it within 15 minutes if your device closes the app in the background.
  • For the request to rate the app: when you first opened the app, on which days you finished a study session, how many sessions you finished, and when the app last asked. The app uses these only to ask rarely and never shows them.

Vocablora does not save your study sessions or their results; it keeps only the counters above. The app reads your device's language list to choose the app language, but does not store that list.

Depending on your device and its backup settings, your device's own backup, such as iCloud or a Google backup, can include the app's data. That backup belongs to your account with Apple or Google, and we cannot see it.

3. Connections to the internet

The app contains everything it needs to study. It makes no internet connection of its own and sends nothing to us or to anyone else, except for actions you start yourself: creating cards with AI (section 6), opening these pages from Settings or the Licences page (section 12) and writing to us (section 8). It has no update check of its own: new versions come through the App Store or Google Play. In the version installed from Google Play, Google adds code that checks that the app was installed from Google Play; this code and the Android components that run inside the app may communicate with Google under Google's policies.

4. The App Store and Google Play

Apple and Google handle downloading, installing and updating the app, under their own privacy policies. If you allow your device to share analytics or diagnostics with app developers (on iPhone in its analytics settings, on Android in its usage and diagnostics setting), Apple or Google may show us crash and performance information in their developer tools: statistics, such as how many devices had a problem, by app version, device model, system version and country or region, and some individual crash reports, which show where in the app's code the problem happened, the device model, the system and app versions and the time. Apple's reports can also carry a code that stays the same for every report from the same device until the device is erased, so these reports are not fully anonymous.

Apple and Google say they remove personal or potentially sensitive information from the reports, and the fields they describe include no name, e-mail address, account or IP address. We use them only to find and fix problems. We know of no setting that lets us stop Apple or Google from collecting them; the setting is on your device. If you test the app through TestFlight, Apple shares crash reports whatever your device settings, and feedback you send through TestFlight reaches us with your name, e-mail address and some device details.

Rarely, the app may ask you to rate it. The rating window belongs to Apple or Google, and what you enter there goes to them under their own policies, not to us.

5. Making decks with a chat service

To add a deck, you can copy a prompt and paste it into a chat service you choose. The prompt contains your chosen languages, the level, fixed instructions and any words you entered or picked from a ready-made list. Vocablora sends nothing to the chat service and never reads your clipboard: you copy and paste yourself. What you type into that service is governed by its own privacy policy and terms.

6. Creating cards with AI

You can optionally create cards by typing your own words in the app. This feature is off by default and requires your permission.

If you tap "Allow" and choose to create a deck or add words with AI, Vocablora sends the words you type, the language you are learning, the meaning language and your level over an encrypted HTTPS connection to a Cloudflare Worker run by the developer, which forwards them to Groq, an AI service, to generate the cards.

Cloudflare sees your device's IP address, because it routes the request. To limit request abuse, our Worker calculates a code from your IP address and the date with a secret key, so the code changes every day. Our Worker gives this code, not your IP address, to Cloudflare's rate limiting, which keeps a count of requests for each code under Cloudflare's terms. The code is not anonymous: whoever holds the key could match it to an IP address. Our Worker code does not write the code, your IP address or your words anywhere, and Worker logs are turned off. Cloudflare processes network traffic under its own privacy policy in the United States and other countries.

Our Worker sends Groq only the words, the languages, the level and fixed instructions; it does not add your IP address or any device identifier. Groq processes them to create the cards. The Zero Data Retention setting is enabled on our account, and Groq states that with this setting it does not retain the inputs and outputs. Groq does keep usage metadata, which it says contains no inputs or outputs, to measure service activity and performance. Under its agreement with us, Groq may not use your words to train models unless we permit or instruct it to, and we have not. Processing may take place in the United States and other countries.

The generated cards are returned directly to your device and saved in the app's local storage. They are never kept on a server by us. Please do not type personal information among your vocabulary words. Before sending, the app checks the words: if one looks like an e-mail address, a web address or a long number such as a phone number, the app names that word and sends nothing. Our Worker runs the same check again: if such a word still reaches it (for example from an older version of the app), the request has already reached Cloudflare and our Worker, but our Worker refuses it and does not pass it on to Groq. Both checks catch only obvious cases; other personal information you type is sent like any other word.

You can turn this feature on or off at any time in Settings under "AI card creation".

7. What we do not do

  • We do not ask you to create an account or sign in.
  • We do not use cookies, analytics, advertising or tracking.
  • We do not sell or share your data.
  • We do not ask for access to your location, camera, microphone, contacts or photos. If you switch on the daily reminder in Settings, the app asks for permission to show notifications; the reminder is scheduled on your device and nothing is sent to us.

8. If you e-mail us

If you write to us, we receive your e-mail address, any name in it, your message and anything you attach. We use them only to answer you: we do not add you to any list or use your message for anything else. Our mailbox is a Gmail account, so Google stores and processes the messages on its servers in the United States and other countries, under its own privacy policy. Please write only what we need to answer you, and do not send files, documents or other people's details unless we ask for them.

We delete a conversation no later than 6 months after its last message, or sooner if you ask. We do this by hand: once a month we delete the conversations whose last message is more than 5 months old and empty Gmail's bin straight away, so they do not wait there for Gmail's automatic deletion after 30 days. After that, Google removes the data from its systems under its own retention policy; Google says this generally takes around 2 months and that its encrypted backups can keep data for up to 6 months.

9. Your choices and rights

  • You can delete a deck at any time in the app.
  • To delete everything, delete the app: this removes its data from your device. A device backup made earlier keeps its copy until that backup is replaced or deleted.
  • Vocablora keeps no copy of your decks and cannot restore lost decks.

Depending on where you live, data protection laws such as the GDPR or Türkiye's KVKK give you rights to access, correct, delete or object to the processing of your personal data. Because your decks never reach us, the personal data we process is limited to the e-mail you send us, the AI card requests described in section 6, which our Worker passes on without saving them, and the crash reports and TestFlight feedback described in section 4, which we view in Apple's and Google's developer tools and do not keep elsewhere. To use your rights, write to us. You can also complain to your data protection authority.

10. Children

Vocablora is intended for people aged 13 and over and is not directed at children. We do not ask for age or date of birth and hold no age data.

11. Transfers abroad

Google, which provides our mailbox, processes e-mails in the United States and other countries. GitHub, which hosts these pages, logs visitors' IP addresses and processes data in several countries, including the United States (section 12). Apple and Google process the data described in section 4 in several countries, including the United States, under their own policies. If you use AI card creation (section 6), the words you type, your chosen languages and level are processed by Cloudflare and Groq, and your IP address only by Cloudflare, in the United States and other countries. This happens only after you allow it in the in-app dialog, and you can withdraw that permission at any time in Settings, which stops further transfers. For users in Türkiye, the KVKK Aydınlatma Metni gives the details.

12. These pages

These pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. When anyone opens them in a browser, GitHub logs and stores the visitor's IP address for security purposes, whether or not the visitor is signed in to GitHub. GitHub handles these logs under its own privacy statement and processes data in several countries, including the United States. We do not receive these logs. In the app, these pages open only when you tap a link to them.

13. Changes

If this policy changes, we update the date at the top. A change to what data leaves your device is described here before the app version that makes it is released.

14. Languages

This policy is also available in Türkçe. If the versions differ, the English version applies, except where the law requires the Turkish version. For users in Türkiye, the KVKK Aydınlatma Metni gives the information the KVKK requires.
